**Court rules facial recognition scans unlawful in landmark case**

LONDON — The Court of Appeal ruled on Tuesday that the automated facial recognition trials conducted by South Wales Police were unlawful, in a defeat for the force and a victory for civil-liberties campaigners.

The judges found that the force had failed to take reasonable steps to determine whether the technology's algorithms were biased, and that its use breached both privacy rights and data-protection law. The Home Office said it would study the judgment "with care".

Ed Bridges, the office worker who brought the case after being scanned at a shopping centre in 2017, called the ruling "a major win for everyone's right to walk down the street without being tracked".

Civil-liberties group Liberty described the judgment as "a watershed moment", warning that "face surveillance has no place on our high streets".

The ruling does not ban the technology outright; whether and how it may be used will now fall to Parliament. Similar challenges are pending in the United States.
